# Kuula — Business lending and investor-funding readiness

## Product mandate
Kuula finances businesses rather than personal consumption. Existing formal
and informal businesses can register. Customers can request working capital,
stock finance, equipment, and business expansion. Requested repayment duration
is 30–365 days; actual availability and the permitted minimum term depend on
risk assessment, regulatory restrictions, and mobile store policies. Never
represent a quote or Growth Line as a guaranteed facility.

## Borrower journey
1. Register account and verify phone and identity.
2. Choose business-purpose financing, desired principal and 30–365 day term.
3. Enter legal/trading name, trading sector, whether registered, registration
   number if applicable, months trading, district/physical location, products
   or services, customer types, and number of workers.
4. Declare monthly business sales, monthly operating expenses, monthly existing
   debt service, specific use of proceeds and repayment plan.
5. Review the full disclosure and submit. The exact snapshot is saved with
   the loan application and visible only within authorized staff workflows.
6. Credit officer reviews documentary/field evidence, records findings and
   explicitly verifies or rejects the business information before an offer is
   eligible for final approval. No automated unverified approval.
7. Loan staff apply the existing credit policy and KYC, acceptance, disbursement,
   settlement and repayment controls. Historical loans remain in the ledger.

## Credit assessment: officer checklist
- Visit or otherwise independently verify physical location and trading activity.
- Verify registration details only if the borrower claims registration.
- Confirm operating tenure and business ownership/authorization.
- Review real evidence of turnover: sales journal, receipts, bank/mobile-money
  statements, supplier invoices and stock purchases with borrower consent.
- Separate gross revenue from profit and document expenses plus existing debts.
- Confirm requested inventory/equipment/business purpose; compare amount to
  trading scale and cash-conversion cycle.
- Assess seasonality, cash flow volatility, repayment coverage, concentration,
  existing arrears and repayment frequency.
- Record findings and evidence reference in Kuula's business verification
  section. A declaration alone never establishes verification.
- Review affordability and applicable law before approving or disbursing.

## Investor-facing narrative
**Problem:** Many viable Ugandan micro and small businesses cannot easily
demonstrate creditworthiness in conventional underwriting, despite operating
cash-flow-positive businesses.

**Solution:** Kuula collects structured business-level data, records a
reviewable underwriting process and provides transparent short-to-medium-term
credit with ongoing loan/portfolio controls.

**Funding proposition:** Investors can evaluate Kuula for a properly
documented wholesale funding line, recoverable revolving credit facility,
portfolio participation or equity financing. These are possible deal structures,
**not** an offer of guaranteed returns or deposits from retail savers.
Structure any transaction with qualified financial/legal advisers and confirm
UMRA, Bank of Uganda, financial-services and investment law implications.

## Evidence required in an investor data room
- Incorporation, ownership, beneficial ownership, applicable lending license
  and legal opinion on the intended funding instrument.
- Signed audited financial statements and management accounts.
- Bank balances, cash-flow forecasts, capital adequacy/borrowings and debt
  obligations; reconciliation to the loan ledger.
- Loan-level *anonymized* vintage and cohort analysis: originations, write-offs,
  collections, recoveries, arrears by days past due, PAR30/PAR90 and defaults.
- Borrower sector/geography/term concentration and underwriting approval rates.
- Cost of capital, customer acquisition cost, cost per active borrower,
  operational cost per loan, risk-adjusted margin and realized yield.
- Lending, affordability, AML/KYC, complaints, privacy/data retention,
  collections and fraud-control policies.
- Independent model validation, recovery controls, backup drills, security
  testing and any known operational incidents and mitigations.
- Borrower consent, privacy controls and independent finance/credit-review
  assurance.

## Dashboard and exports
Admin → Loan Applications → Review business: see borrower-level business
snapshot and record verification evidence in authorized reviewer workflows.
Approvers must not issue new business offers before verification.

Admin → Financial Reports → Funding Overview (No Customer Data):
a PDF with aggregated loan portfolio metrics and monthly performance.
It intentionally excludes names, phone numbers, loan records and case notes.
The existing full loan-level exports remain **internal only**.

Before investor distribution, verify metric denominators and definitions,
remove demo data, reconcile all numbers to actual settled financial records,
and obtain management/legal approval. Never invent traction or publish false
return projections. Historical non-business loans may still be included in
aggregate counts until segregated portfolio reporting is developed.

## Important policy gates
- The code permits terms down to 30 days, but Google Play's short-term loan
  rules may prohibit certain lending app products with repayment due in 60 days
  or less. A business-purpose label is not automatically proof of exemption.
- Legal counsel should confirm the app's exact regulatory category, permissible
  APR, notices and collection practices in each market prior to go-live.
- Do not enable public lending until live settlement, accounting, KYC and
  security controls have been audited and tested with real providers.
